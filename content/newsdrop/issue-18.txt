📜 On a whim
Yuji Tachikawa works on quantum field theory and string theory at the University of Tokyo. One of his collaborative research problems had been stuck for six months. Last week, on a whim, he showed Claude Fable 5 his notes. The model found a calculation error, made what Tachikawa calls “a non-trivial observation” that “essentially solved” the problem, then wrote SymPy code to check its own prediction.
This is not yet a peer-reviewed result, but nevertheless when a physicist of Tachikawa's standing says a model contributed a novel step to live research, it certainly bodes well for AI’s ability to contribute meaningfully to science. → LINK

🆕 Asking the hard questions
This July, 11 cities are hosting Anthropic’s Claude Conversations: open discussions about the hard questions AI raises.
Three questions on the table: What does AI mean for my job? What is AI doing to me, my kids, my community? Who decides the rules?
Two weeks later, each city will complement these sessions with the Claude Impact Lab: full-day hackathons where the communities will try to build their own local solutions. → LINK
Meanwhile Moonshot AI seems to be gearing up to release Kimi K3, rumoured to be an open-weights contender that rivals Fable and Sol-class models on longer-horizon agentic coding tasks. → LINK

🤝 Samsung’s memory windfall
Samsung has pencilled in the biggest operating profit in its history: 89.4 trillion won (about $58 billion) for Q2, up more than nineteen-fold year on year and its third record quarter running.
The engine behind this windfall is memory; HBM and DRAM for the data centres that the hyperscalers are racing to build, driving rising prices across the board.
Yet shares fell as much as 8% after the news. The stock had already doubled this year, revenue came in slightly below estimates, and investors are unsure how long the AI spending boom will hold.
Ironically, rising memory prices may actually be squeezing Samsung's own phone business, which needs to buy the same chips its semiconductor arm sells. → LINK

💙 Corporate Espionage
Last Friday Apple filed a 41-page complaint in a Northern California federal court accusing OpenAI of harvesting its trade secrets to build rival hardware in an elaborate scheme that allegedly ran from junior engineers up to OpenAI's chief hardware officer, himself a 24-year Apple veteran.
Candidates were apparently asked to bring Apple parts to interviews for show-and-tell. Over 400 former Apple employees now work at OpenAI, and Jony Ive's io is a named defendant.
OpenAI denies having untoward interest in other companies' trade secrets. → LINK

🔥 Mind your language
For eighty years we made computers legible by lending them mind-words. Memory, attention, learning, hallucination: all borrowed from human cognition, from von Neumann's 1945 "memory" onwards.
The loan is now flowing back. A linguistic shift is happening as we increasingly use AI jargon to describe our own cognition - our attention spans become akin to context windows, and our life experiences the training data for our own internal world models. Are we humans now undergoing reinforcement learning from machine feedback?
